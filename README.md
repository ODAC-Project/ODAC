# ODAC — Open DAC database

An open, community-maintained database of **digital-to-analogue converters**:
what each one accepts, what it does inside, and the evidence for every claim.

Started at [MozAIk Labs](https://mozaiklabs.fr), who maintain the repository
and the mirror. The project lives under its own name because the data is about
everybody's hardware and should belong to everybody who uses it — see
[Who runs this](#who-runs-this) below. Built in the spirit of
[OPRA](https://github.com/opra-project/OPRA), which solved the same problem for
headphones first.

## Why it exists

Software that chooses a playback setting for you has to know things about your
converter that no driver reports: whether its DSD path is direct, whether it
re-converts DSD to PCM internally, the real depth of its ladder, the level
offset between its PCM and DSD paths. **A probe can tell you what a device
accepts. It can never tell you what the device does with it.**

Of the 27 records this database starts with, **none is complete on what the
device does inside** — seven know the real ladder depth, one knows an internal
bypass rate. That gap does not close by scraping. It closes with people who own
the hardware.

## How to use it — do not clone this repository

The database is published as **one built artefact**, generated by CI. Parsing a
single file is faster than walking a tree, and it keeps traffic off GitHub.
See [docs/CONSUMING.md](docs/CONSUMING.md).

## How it is laid out, and why

```
database/
  devices/<brand>/<device>/
    identity.json          what NAMES the device — never a fact
    claims/NNN-*.json      a batch of facts, with ONE piece of evidence
    open-doubt.json        a doubt worth writing down
  brands/<brand>.json
  chip-families/<family>.json
  interfaces/<interface>.json
  vocabulary/              field definitions, completeness levels, classes
```

**A fact is a file, not a line in a record.** `identity.json` carries only what
names the device; every assertion about it lives in its own claim file with its
own source. Three things follow, and they are the whole point:

- a contribution is a **new file**, so two contributors never conflict;
- a claim **reviews on its own**, instead of as a diff inside a forty-line record;
- two claims that **contradict each other can coexist**, which is what a
  disagreement actually is. Resolution happens at build time, not by overwriting.

## The rules a claim must follow

**No claim without evidence.** Every claim carries a `source` with a `quote` —
the words that carry the fact. A source that does not quote is an address, not
evidence.

**Three states, and `null` is forbidden.** No entry means nobody filled it in.
The string `"unknown"` means the question was asked and nobody knows — and the
consumer takes the conservative branch. These are different facts about where to
spend effort, and one symbol cannot carry both.

**Say whose device it is.** A source that describes a whole range, cited for one
model, is admissible — but it has to say so. It is the most common error in this
data.

## Licence

Following OPRA's split, and for the same reason:

- **Code** (`tools/`, `schemas/`, CI): MIT.
- **Data** (`database/`): **CC BY-SA 4.0**. Commercial use is allowed;
  derivative databases stay available to the community. A code licence does not
  cover the *sui generis* database right in Europe, which is why the data needs
  its own.

## Who runs this

**MozAIk Labs started ODAC and keeps it running.** They set up the repository,
defined the file format, wrote the tooling and the CI, seeded the first records
from their own catalogue, and they host the mirror that serves the built
database so that nobody has to hammer GitHub.

**Why they bother.** MozAIk Labs make Tune, a music server that has to choose a
playback setting for your converter. It cannot do
that well without knowing things no driver reports. They could have kept that
knowledge private; a catalogue of twenty-seven devices kept private is worth
very little, and the same catalogue open has a chance of covering the hardware
people actually own.

**What they do not intend.** They do not intend to decide what this database
becomes. The data is under a licence that lets anyone use it, including
competitors, and the format is plain files in git precisely so that nobody
depends on MozAIk Labs' goodwill to keep using it. If this project outgrows
them, that is a success and not a problem.

**Manufacturers are welcome, and have the most to gain.** Most of what is
missing here is what a device does inside, and the people who know that for
certain are the ones who built it. A manufacturer who fills in their own records
gets their hardware set up correctly by every piece of software that reads this
database — not only Tune.

## Where it stands

27 devices, 298 brands, 9 chip families, 15 interfaces, 48 claims, 6 open
doubts. Nineteen records are complete on what the device accepts; **none** on
what it does inside. The database is seeded from the Tune Smart Upsampler
catalogue, and the projection is checked: no field lost, no value changed.
